## What works

The Garamond-like text, tracked capitals, restrained folio rules, red rubrics, centered mastheads and cream/brown palette make a recognizable identity rather than a generic UI recolor. More is arguably the most convincingly book-like screen: typography and rules do the work without an illustration. Rosary has the strongest single composition; beads meaningfully describe the task instead of merely decorating a list.

## Defects and concrete corrections

1. **Repeated ceremonial headers take too much of a phone viewport.** Bible and Prayers spend around the upper two-fifths on branding, title, illustration and tabs before the real task. Use the tighter candidate as a baseline, then reduce illustration-to-tab whitespace further. Preserve a strong masthead on Home; make utility screens substantially more compact. The differences visible here are improvements, not a major redesign.
2. **Dark-mode hierarchy is weak below the primary text.** Small italic descriptions, navigation labels, chevrons, violet seasonal text and gold rules are muddy against brown. Raise secondary text luminance and give selected navigation the brighter red treatment seen in the winning candidates. Do not solve this by making every ornament brighter. Dark Bible's luminous coral book currently attracts more attention than the content; reduce its intensity and reserve the strongest contrast for words and controls.
3. **The bottom navigation is delicate to a fault.** Labels are tiny tracked capitals and inactive icons faint. Enlarge label text, reduce tracking, and strengthen inactive icon contrast. Keep the ribbon but make the selected icon and label unmistakable; the dark/uncolored selected states in losing candidates do not suffice.
4. **Home has an editorial hierarchy error.** “Violet · Commemoration” reads like a third line of the feast title. Set it as smaller secondary metadata beneath the title, and give the feast name cleaner separation from the season rubric. Purple is also too weak in dark mode. Body reading text is attractive, but the truncated Gospel and low first-reading block show why the header needs discipline.
5. **Ornament is distinctive but unevenly restrained.** The sunburst cross is strong once; broad radiating fans and overlapping candle halos make Bible/Prayers feel illustrated more than typeset. Simplify rays/halos or reduce their scale, especially on repeated visits. The book illustration in light mode has heavy black/red strokes that sit awkwardly beside fine gold rules.
6. **Rosary action hierarchy needs a clear next step.** Keep the play triangle rather than substituting decorative diamonds. The narrow central button and detached circular arrows create three similarly weighted controls; give the primary action stronger contrast and clearer purpose. The reading begins barely above the navigation; reduce the rosary block enough to reveal meaningful prayer text or clearly communicate that more follows below.
7. **List rhythm has unexplained gaps.** Prayers leaves a conspicuous blank band between Intention and the first prayer. Bible's Versions-to-Pentateuch separation is similarly loose. Normalize section spacing so ceremonial pauses occur at real section boundaries. Keep readable row heights; reclaim decorative space first.
8. **Texture and rules need restraint.** The parchment treatment is pleasant, but the whole app risks becoming a single sepia field. Use surface hierarchy and typographic weight to distinguish interactive content. Double rules at section starts plus thin row dividers are adequate; avoid adding further frames or gilding.

I would send both sets back for another pass. The winning candidates improve useful space and state legibility, but no screen yet combines exceptional utility, refined book typography and a compelling visual moment strongly enough for a 9.
