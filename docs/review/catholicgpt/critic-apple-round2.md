## What actually improved, and what still fails

Home A is materially more useful. Moving decoration into a small header tile and surfacing the Mass action gives a complete, readable first-screen task. A further Gospel row becomes visible. It remains visually awkward where the illustration tile meets the purple card with virtually no vertical clearance; it looks attached accidentally. Purple dominates the top third despite being metadata about the day. The excerpt still ends in an inelegant “Now th...” cut. The green Mass button in dark mode is somewhat subdued against the charcoal card. This is a good utility improvement, not a gorgeous composition.

Bible's more compact illustrated header and directly exposed filters make the book library less indirect. The gain is modest because the replacement control stack is still dense: three-way segmented control, search, four pills, count/link row, then a substantial gap before Pentateuch. “Deutero.” looks like developer shorthand and presumes knowledge. Pills are unusually bulky relative to the small count/Versions row, so the control hierarchy lacks refinement. The new rounded header looks better contained but the artwork remains generic radial decoration. Grouped book rows remain legible and well spaced.

Rosary A is substantially better as a prayer screen: the words to pray and playback controls are visible together, with instructions given their own quieter surface. That fixes a real task failure. However, scaling down the same rosary diagram makes the tiny bead field almost decorative noise. The crucifix is minute and the center has unused space. The gap between the diagram and prayer title is awkward; the illustration lacks the precision, tactility, or expressive craft that could make this screen special. Dark mode's ring remains low contrast. The disabled back control is almost invisible. “How to pray” has little hierarchy relative to the instruction copy. Better function, still ordinary artistry.

Prayers' categorized variant is better organized: “Every day” and “Faith & refuge” help scan an otherwise flat library, and exposed intention choices explain the filter's purpose. The horizontal pill row is visually clipped at the right screen edge (“Famil...”); a deliberate peek can signal scrolling, but this hard truncation feels unpolished and is not self-explanatory. Large gaps between groups eat into the density gained from the smaller banner. Long Saint Michael title still crowds the trailing chevron. Header art is smaller but not more distinctive. It is an improvement in editorial organization rather than a standout visual design.

More is functionally unchanged at this scale. Ellipsis is a conventional More symbol, but its tiny footprint against the other heavy tab icons does not improve the overall balance enough to merit a score difference. Both have a clear grouped directory, familiar iOS-like badges, readable descriptions, and a weakly resolved top title/section relationship. The bottom settings surface is cropped by the navigation region; a screenshot alone does not show whether it scrolls fully clear.

## Next three concrete changes

1. **Replace the decorative system with authored, consistently finished artwork.** Remove generic ray banners from Bible/Prayers or use a genuinely purposeful compact header. Give Home's illustration tile breathing room above the feast card. Redraw the rosary with a carefully weighted chain, distinguishable decades and active progress, and a legible crucifix; do not solve this by merely shrinking the existing diagram again.
2. **Refine the control hierarchy and language.** In Bible, make testament scope a compact, fully named control; replace “Deutero.” with a comprehensible label and position the book count/Versions action more deliberately. In Prayers, provide a polished horizontally scrolling filter treatment with padding and a clean edge cue. Reduce arbitrary gaps between control stacks and first content groups.
3. **Calibrate chrome and reading finish.** Harmonize navigation glyph weights and optical sizes, make labels slightly easier to read, tune dark-mode secondary/control contrast, and prevent primary content from appearing stranded beneath the bottom fade. Replace the Home excerpt's mid-word cutoff with a clean line-level truncation or a shorter complete quotation. Keep prayer copy and controls together as in Rosary A.

No winner reaches the brief's exceptional standard. The newer-looking alternatives solve meaningful hierarchy and viewport issues, especially Home and Rosary, but they do not yet demonstrate the distinctive craft, icon consistency, artwork quality, or exact spacing of an outstanding Apple app. Scores improved where the screenshots justify it; there is no wow moment.
