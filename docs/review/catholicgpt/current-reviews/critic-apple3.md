# Apple visual review — latest artwork correction

Rechecked the six latest standalone default Home, Bible, and Prayers Apple renders in both themes under `app/build/screenshots/app/`. Also inspected both current font dialog captures. This follows the blind round-2 review; this final pass is explicitly known-current, not blind. No app source, mapping, edits, or builds inspected/performed.

## Result

**7.6/10 overall visual assessment for these six current renders. No genuine wow and no substantiated AAA claim.** The pages are clear, calm, and more deliberately authored than the earlier boxed-art versions. The free art should stay. It gives Catholic subject matter a recognizable visual presence without covering the controls or prayer text.

| Current screen | Light | Dark |
|---|---:|---:|
| Home | 7.6 | 7.6 |
| Bible | 7.5 | 7.5 |
| Prayers | 7.6 | 7.6 |

The previously obvious dark Home/Bible square and truncated ray field are materially resolved. The cross and book now fade into their backgrounds rather than displaying an apparent image tile. Candles also read as page illustration instead of an app-icon badge. The subjects remain crisp at the displayed size. This directly addresses my highest-priority blind-round objection.

Home has a sensible reading order: day, observance, Mass excerpt/action, then daily practices. The serif scripture treatment contributes character. Bible and Prayers keep the search and content groups understandable. Light/dark themes preserve those relationships well. No text collisions are visible in these six captures. These are screenshot findings, not assertions about touch targets, large text behavior, scroll reachability, or assistive technology.

What keeps this below wow is the mostly conventional grouped-list composition and safe, small art placement. Craft now supports the product, but the pages still feel like the same familiar framework with different content. That is a useful, credible result; removing art would not improve it. The bottom capsule and selected capsule draw substantial attention, and the generous header spacing uses room that could bring more content into view. These are polish opportunities, not evidence of a broken screen.

## Three remaining priorities

1. **Rosary control clarity:** retain the primary Next treatment seen in blind round 2 and give the separate play control a readable audio name. That recommendation is based on the round-2 Rosary screenshots; Rosary was not part of this six-image recheck.
2. **Header and section rhythm:** retain the new transparent art, but test a slightly more compact shared title/art header. The free-art treatment had pushed lower content down in round 2. Improve optical alignment and content visibility without reducing the art to a tiny symbol or shrinking reading text.
3. **Chrome and typographic finish:** make the bottom navigation's enclosing pill less prominent relative to its selected state, and preserve a disciplined sans interface/serif reading distinction. Shared spacing and weight refinements would contribute more than extra cards, dividers, or decorations.

## Font selector

**8/10 visually.** Both themes clearly show the selected Inter radio, Identity default, Roboto, EB Garamond, a live-looking sample sentence, and Done. EB Garamond's label demonstrates a distinct reading texture. The choice is easy to find through More's Font row in round 2. A small Preview label and explicit scope could improve clarity if users might otherwise expect every interface label to change. I did not independently verify actual font application or persistence and do not infer those behaviors from images.
