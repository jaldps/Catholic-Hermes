# Vatican identity — blind round 2

Scope: all ten provided A/B composites and the two separately provided current font-dialog screenshots. Screenshot review only; no app-source or A/B-mapping inspection, no interaction verification, and no live Vatican website comparison. Scores are visual assessments, not measured accessibility compliance.

## Verdict

The identity is coherent, dignified, and recognizable: warm paper, dark ink, rubric red, engraved gold linework, editorial rules, and restrained classical typography belong together. It is substantially more specific than a generic app with a religious illustration pasted on top. The strongest quality is the artwork-to-type relationship in light mode.

It does not yet earn a wow or AAA verdict. I would rate the visible light-mode system **7.6/10** and dark-mode system **7.0/10**. Dark mode loses useful hierarchy and makes navigation and secondary controls too subdued. The long lists are composed well enough, but repeated rules and similarly weighted text remain closer to a good themed catalogue than exceptional editorial craft. The More screen can appropriately remain quiet; adding more artwork would not solve its weaknesses.

The Rosary winner changes sides between the light and dark composites: A-light and B-dark put **NEXT** in the central outlined button and audio in a separate round play control. That arrangement gives the primary manual prayer progression a readable name and the strongest position. The alternatives promote PRAY ALOUD in the central button and leave progression as an isolated arrow. That is less immediately clear for a user praying manually.

## What works

- The light paper tone, muted ink, rubric red, and engraving form a believable prayer-book language. The small engraved ornaments have fine detail without becoming noisy background decoration.
- The Home quotation has comfortable measure, generous leading, and a useful distinction between citation and text. The liturgical title is allowed to breathe.
- Bible and Prayer rows have clear scanning order. Red section rubrics and paired rules introduce real editorial structure.
- The Rosary diagram is unusually relevant: it presents progression and sacred object in one visual, rather than supplying unrelated atmosphere.
- Persistent five-item navigation is understandable. The active bookmark motif is memorable and appropriate to this identity.

## Three priorities

1. **Keep NEXT as the primary Rosary action in both modes.** The explicit word removes uncertainty. Keep audio secondary, and consider giving that icon a short visible Play label if space allows. Preserve generous touch areas; the screenshots do not establish the actual hit boxes.
2. **Raise dark-mode navigation and secondary-control visibility.** Bottom labels, inactive icons, chevrons, gold rules, and small italic metadata become faint at phone scale. Increase their visible size or contrast, while keeping the body text comfortably warm. Active state should remain obvious without depending on red alone. This is a targeted legibility correction, not a request to brighten every decorative line.
3. **Finish the font chooser as part of this identity.** In the supplied dialog, green selection/Done accents, generic sans-serif presentation, and an unexplained Identity default row feel imported from a different app. Use rubric red and the same paper/ink hierarchy, identify the actual default family, and keep the live sentence preview. The font names are useful; the EB Garamond row visibly differs. A static screenshot cannot prove that all choices change the app's actual font family.

## Typography and reading comfort

The contrast between classical display capitals, small rubric labels, italic metadata, and readable serif body copy has the right foundations. The small, widely tracked navigation and tab labels are the weakest typographic tier. Their elegance on a large image should not outweigh readability at normal phone size. More's setting values are readable in light mode but too subdued in dark mode.

Home's primary reading block succeeds. List pages remain easy to scan, but the header/illustration/tab region occupies enough vertical space that only a limited portion of the catalogue is visible. This may be acceptable if it scrolls naturally; static screenshots cannot establish collapse behavior. The partly visible row at the bottom is a normal scroll cue and is not by itself evidence that content is obscured by navigation.

## Font-dialog assessment

Light: **6.0/10** for identity integration, **8.0/10** for straightforward option clarity. Dark: **5.7/10** for identity integration, **7.2/10** for visible readability. Inter is selected and the preview is sans serif; EB Garamond is visibly represented by a serif label. The available choices are concrete and useful. The missing craft is the relationship to the rest of the app and an understandable label for the default.

These are narrow visible findings. There is no basis here to claim a broad A/B visual transformation or verified accessibility compliance.
