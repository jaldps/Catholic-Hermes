All ten randomized pairs inspected as images only. No source/mapping read. Evaluated against the user's new priority: restraint, direct tasks, fewer words, understandable layout. No live Apple Notes or Linear reference was accessible; this is an informed visual judgment, not a verified live comparison.

The restrained alternatives win all ten pairs, for concrete reasons rather than simply having less decoration. Bible exposes more books. Prayers becomes a usable compact library. More fits the whole directory and language state while preserving understandable destination labels. Home reduces ceremonial framing so reading is the clear center. Sans-serif titles fit this utility-led approach better and establish a more consistent hierarchy.

More light B is the most successful screen: destination labels alone are sufficient, and the complete directory fits calmly. It reaches 8 as competent professional work with remaining chrome/typography refinement, not because it is exceptional. Removing descriptions does make broad labels such as Spiritual Life and Habits & Virtues less informative; screenshot review cannot prove that the underlying destination architecture is obvious to first-time users. The old descriptions are not a reason to restore them; clearer destination naming or grouping would be the appropriate fix if discovery testing finds confusion.

No confirmed blocking overlap or horizontal overflow is visible in these pairs. There are still defects that prevent a claim of perfection:

- Home's quotation still ends mid-word (“Now th...”), a reading-quality defect. The Gospel quotation/card and subsequent Gospel of the day row repeat the same passage reference without making their different actions immediately obvious. Daily spiritual routine appears as a stranded heading directly above the bottom chrome. The dark feast-category purple remains subdued.
- Bible still has four control layers before the first book: tabs, search, scope pills, count/Versions. The count-to-section gap feels excessive. “Deuterocanon” is less cryptic than prior shorthand but the relationship of this subset to Old/New is not visually explained. Avoid solving this with a paragraph; resolve the scope architecture.
- Prayers' filter row still clips the Family pill at the right edge. It may scroll, but the abrupt edge is a weak visual finish. The standalone Chaplet card appears detached from the “Other prayers” group for no visible reason. “Other prayers” is a less purposeful category name than the first group's “Every day.” Long Saint Michael row still presses close to its chevron.
- Rosary's Instructions button is a useful reduction, but it is a large isolated secondary control with no clear relationship to the main card. A quieter, consistently aligned disclosure could work better. The diagram still occupies a lot of space for very tiny beads; the active step remains difficult to perceive. Dark disabled back control is extremely faint. The task is clearer, yet visual progress and control hierarchy need work.
- Navigation glyphs still vary in optical weight; the bead icon is busier and the ellipsis smaller than peers. Labels are small. Dark mode secondary labels, chevrons, and ring details have limited visual strength. These are refinements, not evidence of a complete accessibility failure; rendered screenshots cannot verify AX semantics or scale behavior.

Next three changes: (1) fix Home quotation termination and distinguish the reading/Mass/meditation actions through structure; (2) simplify Bible scope architecture and polish Prayers filter edge/grouping; (3) refine Rosary progress readability and secondary disclosure placement, then calibrate common navigation icon weight and dark metadata contrast.

The direction is decisively better aligned with the revised brief. It is not yet “as perfect as Apple Notes/Linear,” and none of the screens genuinely wowed me. Restraint removed obvious distractions; achieving exceptional quality still requires deliberate details and task architecture.
