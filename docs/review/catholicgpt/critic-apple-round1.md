# Apple blind visual review — round 1

Reviewed all ten supplied composite screenshots, solely as images. No source or mapping inspected. No live external reference sites were accessible; the idiom judgment uses my prior knowledge of polished iOS apps. Still screenshots cannot prove target sizes, scrolling, navigation behavior, or motion.

I am not wowed by either version of any screen. Both are competent, legible, fairly consistent interfaces; neither reaches an outstanding Apple app standard. The differences are predominantly a slightly higher content origin and a floating capsule tab bar. That is incremental polish rather than a transformation.

## Shared defects that keep these below the brief

- The header artwork reads as inexpensive generated/vector decoration: radial streaks, small glowing object, and a conspicuous rectangular banner. It consumes roughly a sixth of the viewport on Home, Bible, and Prayers without contributing much functional hierarchy. The candles have mushy glow and the book's red/green illustration feels from a different visual family than the sharp UI glyphs. It is pleasant enough, but not distinctive editorial art.
- Oversized serif headings make a coherent religious/editorial choice, but the overall system has not made that choice feel authored. Heavy sans section labels, utilitarian lists, emoji-like illustration, and the highly rounded purple festival card compete stylistically. Apple quality does not require all-sans typography, but it does require a better resolved relationship among these elements.
- Bottom navigation labels are tiny and the five icons do not have uniform weight or optical size. The rosary is especially fussy compared with the house and grid. The floating variant improves the active state, but the broad capsule and smaller inner active capsule feel thick for the very small label type.
- Both navigation variants fade visible content near the bottom. Scroll-under is ordinary, but here the reader sees a partial action or row with no useful context. Home's primary Mass button is particularly poorly exposed. These screenshots do not demonstrate the resting view of that key action cleanly.
- The large initial top gap is weakly motivated. Raising the content slightly helps, but the difference does not fix the broader vertical economy.
- Dark mode is readable at the primary level but secondary gray text, chevrons, fine separators, and the decorative rosary ring are subdued. The solid black/charcoal treatment is serviceable, not rich or carefully dimensional.

## Screen-specific defects

**Home:** The huge purple card plus equally prominent illustration makes the screen top-heavy. “ORDINARY TIME” and violet commemoration metadata create competing content cues. The Gospel truncates mid-sentence while the action is pushed below the viewport: the screen allocates more space to decorative and ceremonial framing than to completing the immediate reading task. The pale green Mass control has weak visual authority where partially visible. Two different serif scales plus sans metadata yield a crowded typographic stack.

**Bible:** The grouped rows and segmented control are convincingly iOS-like, and the hierarchy is easy to follow. The banner delays search and books unnecessarily. “Books — All” followed by a separate list of books is mildly redundant without additional state context. Section headings are heavier and more generic than the editorial title. The list itself is professional but standard template quality.

**Rosary:** This has the best opportunity for a distinctive interaction but the static execution looks diagrammatic: a huge empty oval, tiny gray beads, a thick washed-out perimeter, and a small flat crucifix. The oval dominates without looking precious or tactile. The selected bead is extremely small; progress would be hard to read. The screen spends most of its space above the actual prayer text, which is pushed under navigation. Play and forward/back controls are clear, but the disabled back control is excessively faint in dark mode. The centered title stack is readable, though four tightly clustered information lines compete with the decorative bead field.

**Prayers:** Clear and efficient lists, sensible search placement. The decorative banner is the main weakness. Long row titles sit close to chevrons; the Saint Michael row feels particularly tight. Latin subtitles are useful but all rendered at the same subdued weight, making the library feel monotonous. “Intention — All” is semantically vague without more explanation, despite occupying its own prominent row.

**More:** Best hierarchy and least decorative interference. Grouped cards, icon badges, and descriptions form a usable directory. It is also visually familiar Settings scaffolding, not a remarkable design achievement. The colored badges could use more deliberate optical consistency; some glyphs feel denser than others. Settings appears partly behind the bottom chrome. More's serif title and heavy sans section labels are acceptable but still not an especially elegant typographic pairing.

## Judgment

The capsule-bar variants win by roughly 0.2 points on average, an intentionally small margin. The active tab is easier to locate and the chrome feels more intentional; neither resolves the screen-level shortcomings. To reach 8+, prioritize better original artwork or a more useful compact header, a more purposeful Home composition, a substantially more refined Rosary visualization, and navigation icon/label calibration. No pair earns 8, and none supplies a genuine wow moment.
